II. Summarizing metric

\subsection{Text Quality Metric Validation}

We evaluated two approaches for predicting human quality ratings using coherence and diversity metrics: (1) normalized differences from human baselines, and (2) distances from empirical population vectors.

\subsubsection{Analysis 1: Human Baseline Approach}
For each generation method within the same dataset, we calculated normalized differences from human-written text:
\begin{align}
\text{coherence\_diff} &= \frac{\text{method\_coherence} - \text{human\_coherence}}{\text{length}} \\
\text{diversity\_diff} &= \frac{\text{method\_diversity} - \text{human\_diversity}}{\text{length}}
\end{align}

These differences were z-normalized across all methods and combined into a mean composite score:
\begin{equation}
\text{mean\_composite} = \frac{z(\text{coherence\_diff}) + z(\text{diversity\_diff})}{2}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Analysis 2: Empirical Distance Approach}
We computed Euclidean distances from empirical reference vectors estimated from 10,000 generations:
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{ref}} &= (-2.782, 0.935) \text{ (coherence, diversity means)} \\
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{ref}} &= (7.218, 0.013) \text{ (coherence, diversity variances)}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Results}
Spearman correlations with human ratings revealed that Analysis 1 significantly outperformed Analysis 2 (Table~\ref{tab:correlations}). The mean composite score from Analysis 1 achieved the strongest correlation (\textit{r} = 0.527, \textit{p} < 0.001), indicating that methods generating more diverse content than human baselines receive higher quality ratings.

These findings suggest that text quality is best predicted by comparing generation methods to human-written baselines rather than absolute population statistics, with diversity improvements being particularly important for perceived quality.

\section{Principal Component Analysis of Text Quality Metrics}

We conducted a Principal Component Analysis on four automated text quality metrics: coherence mean, coherence variance, diversity mean, and diversity variance. The analysis aimed to identify underlying dimensions of text quality and assess their relationship with human evaluations.

\subsection{Dimensional Structure}

The PCA revealed a clear two-dimensional structure underlying the text quality metrics. The first two principal components accounted for 88.76\% of the total variance in the data (Table~\ref{tab:pca_summary}). PC1 explained 58.23\% of the variance, while PC2 contributed an additional 30.53\%. The remaining components (PC3: 8.87\%, PC4: 2.38\%) captured minimal variance, suggesting that text quality can be effectively represented in a reduced two-dimensional space.

PC2 & 1.217 & 30.53 \\

\midrule
Cumulative (PC1+PC2) & -- & 88.76 \\
\bottomrule

\end{table}

\subsection{Component Interpretation}

The variable loadings revealed distinct interpretations for each component. PC1 was primarily driven by diversity metrics, with diversity mean (34.9\% contribution) and diversity variance (33.3\% contribution) as the dominant loadings, alongside coherence mean (25.5\% contribution). This component appears to capture overall text variability and richness.

PC2 was dominated by coherence variance (60.3\% contribution), with smaller contributions from coherence mean (16.4\%) and diversity variance (13.1\%). This component primarily reflects text consistency and coherence stability.

\subsection{Correlation with Human Ratings}

Spearman correlations between principal component scores and human ratings revealed significant relationships (Table~\ref{tab:correlations}). PC2 showed the strongest association with human judgments ($\rho = -0.48$, $p < 0.001$), indicating that texts with lower coherence variance (i.e., more consistent coherence) received higher human ratings. PC1 also demonstrated a significant negative correlation ($\rho = -0.36$, $p < 0.001$), suggesting that certain patterns of diversity and coherence negatively predict human preferences.

\subsection{Implications}

The results demonstrate that automated text quality metrics can be effectively reduced to two primary dimensions that collectively explain nearly 90\% of the variance. The significant negative correlations of both PC1 and PC2 with human ratings suggest that human evaluators prefer texts with specific patterns of coherence and diversity. Notably, PC2's strong correlation indicates that consistency in coherence (low coherence variance) is a key predictor of human text quality judgments, explaining approximately 23\% of the variance in human ratings ($r^2 = 0.48^2 = 0.23$).

These findings provide valuable insights for automated text evaluation systems, suggesting that focusing on coherence consistency and controlled diversity patterns may better align with human quality assessments than individual metric optimization.
